# Lessons from the ten-round Seaquest study

Recorded 2026-09-25 at the owner's request. This is a retrospective synthesis of
the [completed study](ten-round-results-2026-09-24.md), not a new experiment or a
change to its frozen protocol. The ten-round allocation remains closed.

We learned that an explicit question policy can improve bounded Seaquest scores,
and that policy quality and reliable execution of the written rule need separate
measurements. We have not established a repeatable teacher optimizer.

## What the evidence supports

### 1. Simple, explicit control rules improved held-out performance

Training mean return rose from 70 for the collapsed DOWN/NOOP baseline to 190
with firing/depth conditions and 350 with the added enemy-facing rule. That
round-three version was selected before development and kept fixed thereafter:

| Split | Baseline mean | Selected policy mean | Paired mean gain |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Development, seeds 326/327 | 80 | 370 | +290 |
| Final, seeds 328/329 | 80 | 290 | +210 |

Neither seed regressed in either split. Both prospective gates passed. This
supports bounded improvement over this baseline under the recorded start/horizon
contract, beyond the two training starts. Two seeds per split do not establish
population performance, and the comparisons do not isolate individual clauses.
See the [paired results](../../experiments/seaquest/ten-round-v1-continuation/final-metrics.json).

### 2. A more elaborate strategy was not automatically a better executable policy

Always seeking a diver while empty reduced training mean return from round
three's 350 to 160. Explicit facing wording partially recovered to 260; delaying
diver seeking until lower oxygen reached 280. None replaced the simpler winner.

The records contain both questionable resource-management behavior and failures
to follow the intended rule. A sensible-sounding strategy can therefore fail
because of its design, its execution, or both. The hypothesis that additional
conditions caused the execution errors remains unconfirmed: revisions also
changed the states encountered. These were not matched-state complexity tests.

### 3. Aggregate rule agreement hid movement and turning errors

The round-three question matched its literal rule on 1,490/1,600 visited training
states (93.1%). Excluding opportunities whose intended action was FIRE or NOOP
left only 129/175 matches (73.7%). The corresponding movement/turning figures were
119/740 (16.1%) for round four, 196/573 (34.2%) for round five, and 113/675 (16.7%)
for round eight.

Frequent, simple actions can dominate the aggregate metric. Future diagnostics
should report branch-specific counts as well as the total. These labels measure
execution of the written rule, not optimal play; the different visited-state
distributions prevent a causal comparison of wording from these percentages alone.
The [branch counts](../../experiments/seaquest/ten-round-v1-continuation/literal-branch-diagnostic.json)
and [checker method](../../experiments/seaquest/ten-round-v1-continuation/literal-rule-check-final-method.txt)
preserve the denominators and assumptions.

### 4. Short capped scores did not establish sustainable resource management

Some loaded ascents replenished oxygen without immediate life loss. Empty ascents
also preceded animation and life loss; the adapter does not independently label
the cause of death. The maximum recorded carried count was two, with no full
six-diver rescue demonstrated. Both final candidate episodes reached the cap
and lost two lives each.

Round-seven seed 321 lost no lives before the cap but ended empty with oxygen 25.
A later return threshold may postpone a failure beyond the measurement window;
the experiment did not observe that counterfactual continuation. Report ending
state, native termination and capped survival alongside score. The
[horizon diagnostic](../../experiments/seaquest/ten-round-v1-continuation/records/training-horizon-diagnostic.json)
records these examples without converting them into proof of sustainable play.

## What we have not learned yet

Jev weights were fixed. The interactive coordinator authored seven revisions
using training feedback, and later revisions also used source-reported game
mechanics. There were zero isolated teacher calls, no no-feedback proposal arm
and no independent optimizer replications. The [proposal log](teacher-log.md)
records that provenance. Improved scores do not establish that feedback itself
caused the improvement, that Jev learned new weights, or that an automated teacher
can repeatedly find better policies. Mastery and transfer to another game also
remain unestablished.

## The next discriminating question

Proposed, not executed: **Can Jev reliably execute the intended rule when the
observed game state is held fixed?**

Use training-only observations and the same state/history/action options for each
wording variant. Freeze state sampling and rule labels before model evaluation,
and report errors by branch, especially return, collection and facing conflicts.
Rule agreement would diagnose execution rather than optimality. Separately,
matched local literal-controller rollouts could test the intended strategy's
behavior; they would be controls, not a substitute for Jev during model episodes.

If the literal controller also performs poorly, the strategy deserves revision.
If its performance is stronger while Jev repeatedly chooses different actions,
question execution becomes a concrete target. Neither outcome is established yet.
A causal teacher-feedback study would additionally need matched proposal budgets,
a no-feedback comparison and independent replications. Any further policy tuning
must use training data, with fresh held-out seeds and a separately frozen budget;
the completed development/final results are not proposal inputs.

This note records lessons and proposed distinctions only. It launched no model
calls, reopened no allocation and changed no original result or protocol.

## Subsequent five-round follow-up

The owner subsequently authorized the [five-round execution study](execution-five-round-results-2026-09-25.md).
It is now complete: the selected same-rule wording revision failed both final
gates. The new report adds evidence that literal strategy and model execution
can rank differently, branch repairs can damage other branches, and balancing
samples by action alone does not balance their conditional-branch composition.
The original retrospective claims above retain their earlier evidence scope.
